# Research basics

## A hypothesis comes before a result

A research hypothesis describes a possible relationship and why it might exist. A strong historical result is evidence to investigate, not proof that the explanation is correct or that the relationship will persist.

Before testing, write down the question, the data needed and what would count as evidence against the idea.

## Separate development from evaluation

Use an earlier time period to develop an idea and a later period to evaluate it. Keep chronological order: random shuffling can leak information from the future into a time-dependent exercise.

Once I inspect an evaluation period and change the idea because of it, that period has influenced development. It is no longer untouched validation.

## Compare more than one number

Historical return alone misses risk and trading activity. Ask how uneven returns are, how large declines become, whether behavior changes across periods, and whether costs could overwhelm the result.

Similar implementations can share the same underlying economic idea. Counting variants is not the same as finding independent evidence.

## Open question

How can I design a small synthetic example where selecting the best of many random trials produces an impressive but misleading result?
